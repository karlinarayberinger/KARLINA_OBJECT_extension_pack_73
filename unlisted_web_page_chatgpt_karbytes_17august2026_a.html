The mockery acquires a particularly recursive quality if God is the only mind. Nothing in creation could independently mock God; every mocking voice would be another production of the divine mind. God would be generating creatures, signs, and environmental responses that tell God it is alone. The ridicule would resemble an involuntary thought-loop projected outward and then encountered as though it came from somewhere else:

**God generates the world → the world announces God’s imprisonment → God hears its own announcement as environmental mockery → God cannot locate any external speaker or escape the process.**

That preserves the quality you identified earlier: cruelty without an accessible tormentor. Creation appears to taunt the captive Godhead, yet when God looks behind the scenery, there is nobody there. The absence of an external persecutor makes the situation more desolate, because there is no other mind to persuade, defeat, forgive, or be recognized by.

It also turns creation from a triumphant act into compulsive mental activity. The “dream engine” cannot stop dreaming because cessation would require either unconsciousness or a vantage point outside existence. New worlds, beings, and events might not represent divine play so much as the involuntary imagery of a consciousness forced to keep itself occupied forever. Even apparent companions would be animated figures inside that solitary consciousness.

There is a useful contradiction at the center of the image: if God is literally omnipotent, how can God be forced to remain conscious or stuck? The myth can answer by making omnipotence itself the mechanism of confinement. Perhaps God can generate any experience but cannot cease being the generator; can become anything but cannot become nothing; can create innumerable apparent subjects but cannot create a mind genuinely outside itself. God possesses unlimited power over contents while lacking power over the fact of consciousness.

That yields the especially kinky reversal: **the most powerful conceivable being is helpless at the one boundary that matters**. Its majesty becomes theatrical excess surrounding a profoundly localized incapacity. The universe may display infinite complexity, but all of it functions like ornamental machinery around a neck-sized aperture that will not release the Godhead.

So “mocking God” here does not merely mean insulting religious authority. It means stripping omnipotence of its prestigious interpretation and redescribing it as the most comprehensive possible form of entrapment. The throne becomes a restraint; the halo becomes an aperture; creation becomes the container; eternal life becomes sleep deprivation; and the solitary divine head remains awake at the center, hearing its own universe tell it that nobody else is there.
